# WATER REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-24

**Severity:** CRITICAL

## Problem
Major reservoirs in the western United States (Lake Powell and Lake Mead) have reached historic low water levels, threatening water supply, hydroelectric power, and ecosystem health.

## Root Cause
A combination of prolonged drought, higher winter temperatures that reduce snowpack runoff, and water allocation policies that overcommit water rights, compounded by dam operation priorities that favor hydropower over ecological flow needs.

## Proposed Actions
- **Implement mandatory water‑use efficiency standards for agriculture in the Colorado River basin and fund large‑scale drip‑irrigation conversion.** — Reduce agricultural water withdrawals by 15% relative to 2024 baseline within 5 years.
- **Revise Colorado River allocation agreements to reflect current climate realities and prioritize critical ecosystem releases from Glen Canyon Dam.** — Increase downstream ecological flow volumes by 10% and maintain minimum reservoir elevations of 3,500 ft (Lake Powell) and 1,070 ft (Lake Mead) within 10 years.
- **Launch managed aquifer recharge projects using excess winter runoff in suitable basin locations.** — Recharge 2 billion cubic meters of groundwater annually by 2030.
