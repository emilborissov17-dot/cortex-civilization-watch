# ECOSYSTEMS BIODIVERSITY REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-21

**Severity:** HIGH

## Problem
There is no coordinated global authority for wildlife health, resulting in fragmented monitoring and delayed responses to emerging disease threats that harm biodiversity.

## Root Cause
International wildlife health responsibilities are split among national agencies, NGOs, and research institutions without a binding global governance framework, leading to insufficient data sharing, funding, and rapid response capacity.

## Proposed Actions
- **Establish a UN‑backed Global Wildlife Health Authority with a legal mandate for data collection, disease surveillance, and coordinated emergency response across member states.** — By 2028, achieve real‑time reporting of wildlife disease outbreaks in at least 80% of countries and conduct coordinated response in 5 major disease events.
- **Create an open, interoperable wildlife health data platform linking national agencies, NGOs, and researchers.** — Platform operational with contributions from at least 100 institutions and >10,000 validated records within 3 years.
