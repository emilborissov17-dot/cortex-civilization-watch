# GOVERNANCE INSTITUTIONS REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-05

**Severity:** HIGH

## Problem
Migrants in distress at sea experience prolonged exposure due to fragmented international coordination and lack of real-time vessel tracking, exemplified by a boat adrift off the Canary Islands for almost a month before being found.

## Root Cause
Governance gaps: no mandatory global requirement for AIS/ELT devices on migrant vessels, limited data sharing between coastal states, NGOs, and UN agencies, and insufficient rapid-response maritime mechanisms.

## Proposed Actions
- **Adopt a UN resolution mandating AIS or equivalent emergency beacon installation on all vessels carrying migrants, with subsidies for NGOs operating rescue boats.** — 99% of registered migrant transport vessels equipped with tracking devices within 3 years.
- **Create a UN‑led Rapid Maritime Response Task Force that can be deployed within 24 hours to any reported distress signal in international waters.** — Average time from distress report to rescue reduced from 30 days to <48 hours within 2 years.
- **Establish a shared, real‑time database (accessible to coast guards, NGOs, and UN agencies) logging all migrant vessel movements and distress calls.** — Database operational with >80% coverage of Mediterranean and Atlantic migration routes within 5 years.
