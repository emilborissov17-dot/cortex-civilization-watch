# MATERIALS WASTE REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-18

**Severity:** HIGH

## Problem
Rapid expansion of data centers is projected to generate a massive increase in discarded electronic equipment, creating a 'tsunami' of toxic e‑waste within the next 25 years.

## Root Cause
Unchecked growth of data center capacity combined with short product lifecycles and insufficient circular‑economy mechanisms for servers, storage, and networking gear.

## Proposed Actions
- **Adopt mandatory EPR legislation for all data‑center class electronic hardware, requiring manufacturers to take back, refurbish, or recycle end‑of‑life equipment.** — Achieve a 40% reduction in net e‑waste generated per new gigawatt of data‑center capacity by 2030, measured through national e‑waste reporting.
- **Create a certification program for data‑center operators that rewards the use of modular, upgradable, and recyclable server designs.** — At least 30% of new data‑center deployments globally receive the certification by 2028.
- **Establish a public‑private fund (e.g., leveraging state grants like Massachusetts' $2.8M waste‑reduction grants) to finance large‑scale e‑waste collection and advanced recycling facilities focused on high‑value components such as lithium‑ion batteries.** — Process 800,000 tons of data‑center related e‑waste annually by 2035, with ≥70% material recovery rate.
