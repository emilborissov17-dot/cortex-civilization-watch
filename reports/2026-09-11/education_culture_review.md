# EDUCATION CULTURE REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-11

**Severity:** HIGH

## Problem
Unregulated AI-generated video content (often termed 'baby slop') is being used in early childhood education, distorting learning outcomes and increasing cognitive overload for young children.

## Root Cause
Rapid proliferation of AI video tools combined with a lack of clear guidelines, media‑literacy curricula, and platform accountability leads schools and parents to adopt low‑quality AI content without assessing its educational value.

## Proposed Actions
- **Develop and mandate an AI Media Literacy module for early‑childhood educators and caregivers, including criteria for evaluating AI video quality and relevance.** — By the end of the first school year of implementation, 90% of participating teachers report confidence in vetting AI content, and a 30% reduction in reported use of unvetted AI videos in classrooms.
- **Require major AI video platforms to label content intended for children with a standardized quality seal and to enforce stricter age‑appropriate moderation.** — Within 12 months, 80% of platforms adopt the seal, and the number of flagged ‘baby slop’ videos drops by at least 50%.
- **Create a public repository of vetted, open‑access AI‑generated educational videos aligned with early learning standards.** — Repository hosts at least 500 high‑quality videos within 18 months, with usage analytics showing a 40% shift from unvetted to vetted resources.
