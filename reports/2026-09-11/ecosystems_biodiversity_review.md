# ECOSYSTEMS BIODIVERSITY REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-11

**Severity:** HIGH

## Problem
Conservation research on tropical insects is dominated by 'parachute science', leading to inadequate protection of the >80% of insect species that reside in the tropics.

## Root Cause
External researchers conduct studies without meaningful involvement of local scientists, resulting in data gaps, misaligned priorities, and insufficient capacity building in tropical regions.

## Proposed Actions
- **Create a Global Tropical Biodiversity Research Fund that requires at least 50% of principal investigators to be based in the study country and allocates dedicated capacity‑building grants for local institutions.** — Increase the proportion of peer‑reviewed tropical insect studies with local lead authors from current ~20% to ≥60% within five years.
- **Mandate that research permits for biodiversity work in tropical nations include a Local Collaboration Clause, obligating foreign teams to partner with accredited local researchers and share all raw data openly.** — Achieve 100% compliance of new permits with the clause within two years, tracked via permit databases.
- **Launch a Global Wildlife Health Authority pilot program focused on insect health monitoring, integrating local field stations and citizen‑science networks.** — Establish continuous monitoring sites in at least 30 biodiversity hotspots covering 25% of known tropical insect diversity within ten years.
