# SOCIAL RELATIONS REVIEW
**Date:** 2026-09-11

**Severity:** HIGH

## Problem
A large and growing proportion of U.S. adults report having no close friends they can rely on in a crisis, indicating widespread social isolation and loneliness.

## Root Cause
Erosion of traditional community structures combined with increased reliance on digital interactions, geographic mobility, and limited public spaces for informal social contact.

## Proposed Actions
- **Launch a nationwide "Social Prescription" program where primary‑care providers refer patients who screen positive for loneliness to vetted community groups and activities.** — Reduce the percentage of adults reporting zero close friends from the current level to under 20% within five years, measured by annual Pew Research surveys.
- **Allocate federal and municipal funding to create or upgrade local community hubs that host low‑cost, interest‑based meet‑ups (e.g., hobby clubs, intergenerational programs).** — Establish at least one active hub in 80% of U.S. census tracts within three years; track attendance and repeat participation rates.
